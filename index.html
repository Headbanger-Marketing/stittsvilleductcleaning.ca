Listing accessories attached to the ducts such as a humidifier or air cleaner

For any Stittsville request, start with the rooms, registers and equipment to be cleaned. A humidifier, a media or electronic air cleaner or a UV lamp may also be fitted to the ducts or the furnace cabinet without showing from the rooms. If your home has any of these, list each one, where it sits and whether it is in use. Copy any label or model you can read from outside, and gather service paperwork. The provider who responds can then say how each item is treated during cleaning.

  1. Return grilles

    Openings that pull room air back into the system.

  2. Return ducts

    Carry that air back to the furnace.

  3. Furnace blower and coil

    Blower assembly, coil and the cabinet around them.

  4. Supply trunk

    The main duct leaving the furnace.

  5. Branch runs

    Smaller ducts feeding each room.

  6. Supply registers

    Where air comes back into each room.

  • Labels and models

    Photograph any readable label or model name on the outside of each accessory, without opening a cover or panel.

  • Use and paperwork

    Note whether each item is running, switched off or unused, and collect any manual, invoice or service record you hold.

Questions About Your Stittsville Request

Is Stittsville Duct Cleaning a company that cleans ducts itself?

Because it owns no vehicles, employs no cleaners and does not carry out cleaning, this is an enquiry website for Stittsville, not a contractor. Once our enquiry team has reviewed a request, the team may pass it to a provider. Before any work is arranged or priced, that provider must tell you who they are and confirm that your address falls within their coverage. Nothing is booked through the site.

How far does a Stittsville inquiry go once you send it?

Sending an inquiry takes it as far as review: your description and photos are placed in front of our enquiry team. Sending it books no visit, confirms no coverage, fixes no price and chooses no provider. You and the provider who responds agree those points afterwards, in writing, and you can decline at any stage. Photos of labels and connections help the provider understand what you describe.

Which accessories should I list for my Stittsville duct system?

List anything attached to the ducts or the furnace cabinet that you know of: a humidifier, a media or electronic air cleaner, a UV lamp or a similar device. Say where each one sits, whether it is in use, and what any visible label reads. If you are unsure what a part is, describe it and add a photo rather than guessing.

Do I have to open a panel to find the model of an accessory?

No. Read only what is visible from outside, and leave every cover, door and panel closed. If no label can be read, say so in the inquiry. The provider can establish the make and model if a visit is arranged, and anything involving gas, electrical or control questions needs appropriately qualified diagnosis rather than an owner's guess.

Does a humidifier or air cleaner change how a provider cleans the ducts?

It can change the plan, which is why the request should list it. A provider may need to work around an attachment, leave it untouched or follow the manufacturer's instructions for it. No accessory is assumed to need cleaning, adjusting or replacing, and the written scope should say how each one is treated. Ask for that in writing before agreeing.

Can one Stittsville request cover the ducts and the dryer vent together?

Yes, a single inquiry can describe both, but treat them as two lines of work. Each should have its own scope, record and price if the provider agrees to both. Keep the dryer details and the accessory list under separate headings.

What should I say if a UV lamp is fitted and I do not know whether it works?

Say exactly that: a lamp is fitted and you cannot tell whether it is in use. Note where it is, photograph any visible label or housing from outside, and add any paperwork you hold. Do not switch it on or off to find out. The provider can then ask the right questions and record its condition as found.

Who can approve cleaning around an accessory in a rented or shared building?

The person who controls the equipment, which may be the owner, the landlord, the property manager or a condominium corporation. Name that person in the inquiry, along with who pays and who can give access. Lease terms are a question for the landlord or a lawyer. The provider should not work around an accessory until that person has agreed to the written scope.
